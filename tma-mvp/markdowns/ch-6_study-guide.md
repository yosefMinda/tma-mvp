# Unit 6 Study Guide: Africa and the Outside World (1500–1880s)

## 2\. Early European Contacts & Aims (15th–16th C)

The Portuguese initiated direct maritime trade with West and Central African states.

```
[PORTUGUESE EXPANSION TIMELINE]
 1415: Conquest of Ceuta (Morocco)
   │
 1432: Reached Cape Bojador 
   │
 1460: Shore of Sierra Leone
   │
 1482: Built Elmina Castle (Gold Coast to secure gold/captives)
```

### 🎯 The Three Core Portuguese Objectives

1. **Commercial:** Circumventing Muslim middle-men on the trans-Saharan routes to access West African gold fields directly.
2. **Religious:** Finding the mythical Christian king **"Prester John"** to form an alliance against Ottoman naval forces.
3. **Geopolitical:** Exploring a maritime route around Africa to gain monopoly control of the Indian Ocean spice trade.

## 3\. The Slave Trade & Its Demise

### 🔄 The Triangular Trade System

- **Leg 1: Europe to Africa:** Manufactured goods (guns, ammunition, textiles, and alcohol) were shipped to Africa.
- **Leg 2: The Middle Passage (Africa to the Americas):** Millions of captured Africans were transported under brutal, unhygienic conditions. Between 10 and 12 million Africans survived this passage.
- **Leg 3: The Americas to Europe:** Plantations shipped raw products (sugar, cotton, tobacco) back to European factories.
```
[EUROPE]
                  /      \
    Guns & Cloth /        \ Sugar, Cotton & Tobacco
                v          \
            [AFRICA] ----> [THE AMERICAS]
                    Enslaved People 
                   (Middle Passage)
```

### 🛑 Abolition (Act of 1807)

- **Economic Drive:** The **Industrial Revolution** shifted European commercial priorities from human labor to raw agricultural materials and machinery.
- **Humanitarian Drive:** Activists exposed the brutal realities of the Middle Passage as a crime against humanity.

### 📉 Impact on Africa

- **Demographic Stagnation:** The forced removal of millions of young, productive individuals disrupted local population growth and created massive gender imbalances.
- **Economic Stagnation:** Local manufacturing collapsed under the flood of cheap European imports; state activities shifted from farming to slave raiding.
- **Political Disruption:** Fueled civil wars and gave rise to militarized predatory states (e.g., Dahomey) that collapsed when the trade ended.

## 4\. Legitimate Trade & The Transition

**"Legitimate Trade"** refers to the trade in natural commodities that replaced the slave trade in the 19th century.

- **Essential Commodity:** **Palm Oil** became the primary West African export (especially from the Niger Delta) used to lubricate European industrial factory machinery.
- **Social Impact:** The trade enabled small-scale farmers and merchants to prosper, undermining the monopolies of regional kings and slave-raiding chiefs.
- **Path to Colonization:** To maximize profits, European companies (such as the Royal Niger Company) bypassed African middlemen and pushed into the interior, laying the groundwork for imperial colonization.

## 5\. White Settlement in South Africa

```
1652: Dutch VOC sets up strategic refreshment station at Cape of Good Hope
   │
 1659 & 1673-77: Khoisan-Dutch Wars (Khoisan lose grazing lands; weakened by smallpox)
   │
 1795: British seize control of the Cape Colony
   │
 1830s: Great Trek (Boers flee British rule, land reforms, and slave abolition)
   │
 Establish: Boer Republics (Transvaal & Orange Free State) in the interior
```

### ⚔️ The Great Trek Conflict

- The northward migration of Dutch-descendant **Boers** (Afrikaners) brought them into direct conflict with powerful regional kingdoms like the **Xhosa** and **Zulu**.
- Indigenous peoples were pushed into marginal lands, establishing a template for cheap migrant labor in South Africa.

## 6\. European Explorers & Missionaries (1770–1870)

### 🧭 Exploration Highlights

- **The Drivers:** Driven by scientific societies (like the **African Association** founded in 1788) looking to map major rivers (Niger, Congo, Nile, Zambezi) for future trade routes.
- **The Breakthrough:** The discovery of **Quinine** in the 1850s reduced malaria mortality rates, making interior exploration safe for Europeans.
- **Key Figures:**
	- **David Livingstone:** Mapped the Zambezi river, combining exploration with missionary work.
		- **Henry Morton Stanley:** Proved the navigability of the Congo River, which directly inspired King Leopold II of Belgium to colonize the Congo basin.
		- **Burton & Speke:** Documented the source of the White Nile at Lake Victoria.

### ⛪ The Missionary Dual Role

While establishing schools and introducing western medicine and literacy, missionaries often requested military protection from their home governments during local political conflicts, directly facilitating the military partition of Africa in the 1880s.

**🎯 Exam Tip:** Always connect the **Industrial Revolution** with both the **Abolition of the Slave Trade** and the rise of **Legitimate Trade**. Remember that the **Rozwi** successfully expelled the Portuguese in the 1690s, while the **Boers** carried out the **Great Trek** to escape British rule.
