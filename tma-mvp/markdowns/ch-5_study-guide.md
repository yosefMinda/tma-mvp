# Unit 5 Study Guide: Peoples and States of Africa to 1500



## 1\. African Languages: Classification & Distribution

Africa is incredibly diverse, boasting between 1,000 and 2,000 distinct indigenous languages. Linguists group these into four major "super-families":

| Language Super-Family | Key Sub-Groups & Languages | Geographic Region |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1\. Niger-Congo** | Includes **Bantu** (Zulu, Swahili, Shona), Yoruba, Wolof. (Largest family by speaker count) | West, Central, East, and Southern Africa |
| **2\. Afro-Asiatic** | Semitic (Amharic, Arabic, Tigrinya), Cushitic (Oromo, Somali, Afar), Omotic, Berber | North Africa, Sahara, and the Horn of Africa |
| **3\. Nilo-Saharan** | Kanuri, Dinka, Luo | Parts of Central, Eastern, and Northeastern Africa |
| **4\. Khoisan** | Known for distinct "click" consonant sounds (San and Khoi-Khoi) | Southern Africa (Kalahari region) |

### 🔑 Key Concept: The Swahili Language & Culture

- **Origin:** Developed along the East African coast as a hybrid of indigenous **Bantu** syntax and heavy vocabulary borrowings from **Arabic** and Persian due to Indian Ocean maritime trade.
- **Status:** Serves as a major *lingua franca* (common trade language) across East Africa (Tanzania, Kenya, Uganda).

## 2\. North Africa & Mamluk Egypt

### ⚔️ The Mamluk Sultanate (1250–1517 AD)

- **Who were they?** "Mamluk" literally means "owned" or "slave." They were a elite military class of Turkish/Circassian origin serving under the Egyptian **Ayyubid Dynasty** (founded by Saladin).
- **Seizure of Power (1250):** Overthrew their masters during the chaos of the Sixth Crusade.
- **Great Military Achievements:**
	1. **Defeated the Crusaders:** Captured King Louis IX of France.
		2. **Halted the Mongols (1260 AD):** Decisively defeated the Mongol army at the **Battle of Ain Jalut**, protecting Egypt and North Africa from destruction.
- **Decline:** Defeated in 1517 AD by the **Ottoman Empire**, which used advanced gunpowder technology (muskets and cannons), whereas the Mamluks still relied heavily on traditional cavalry.

### 🕌 The Arab Conquest & Berber Dynasties

- **Conquest of Egypt (642 AD):** Muslim Arab armies seized Egypt from the Byzantine Empire, using it as a stepping stone to expand westward into the **Maghrib** (Libya, Tunisia, Algeria, Morocco).
- **Berber Response:** The indigenous Berbers initially resisted Arab political control but gradually converted to Islam.
- **Berber Empires:** By the 11th century, reformist Berber movements like the **Almoravids** and **Almohads** established powerful empires that spanned Northwest Africa and Southern Spain (Al-Andalus).

### 🕌 Islam and West African Scholarship

- Unlike North Africa, Islam in West Africa was spread peacefully via **merchants and scholars** rather than military conquest.
- **Timbuktu:** Became the intellectual heart of West Africa, hosting the famous **University of Sankore**, which attracted scholars, doctors, and jurists from all over the Mediterranean world.

## 4\. Central, Eastern, and Southern Africa

### 👑 Central Africa: Kingdom of Kongo

- Highly centralized state ruled by the **Manikongo** (King).
- Divided into organized provinces led by governors appointed directly by the king.
- Developed a robust monetary system utilizing cowry shells (*nzimbu*) and excelled in ironwork.

### ⚓ Eastern Africa: The Swahili Port Cities

- Independent maritime city-states like **Mombasa**, **Kilwa**, and **Zanzibar** flourished along the coast.
- Used sailing vessels called **Dhows** to trade with Arabia, India, and China.
- Imported porcelain and silk; exported African raw materials (gold, ivory, iron).

### 🧱 Southern Africa: Great Zimbabwe

- Built by the Bantu-speaking **Shona** people starting around 1000 AD.
- **Architecture:** Famous for its massive **dry-stone walls** (constructed without mortar). The structures served as a royal palace and religious center.
- **Economy:** Wealth was derived from massive cattle herds and controlling the flow of gold from inland mines to the Swahili coastal ports.

## 5\. Intra- and Inter-Continental Relations

### 🐪 The Trans-Saharan Caravan Trade

- **The Medium:** The introduction of the **Camel** (around the 4th century AD) revolutionized desert travel, enabling heavy cargo crossings.
- **Exchange:** Linked the Mediterranean/North African economies with Sub-Saharan West Africa, triggering urban growth and cultural exchange.

### 👤 Social Origins of the Slave Trade

- **Internal:** Prior to European contact, forms of slavery/servitude existed in many African societies as a result of tribal warfare, judicial punishment, or debt.
- **External Demand:** The expansion of Trans-Saharan caravan routes and Indian Ocean trade networks significantly widened the demand for enslaved labor, shipping captives to North Africa, Persia, and the Middle East long before the Transatlantic Slave Trade began.

**🎯 Exam Tip:** Be prepared to compare the spread of Islam in North Africa (military conquest) with its spread in West Africa (peaceful trade and elite conversion). Always remember that the **Mamluks** saved the region from the **Mongols** at **Ain Jalut (1260 AD)**.
