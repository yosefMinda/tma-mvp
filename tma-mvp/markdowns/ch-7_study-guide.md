# Unit 7 Study Guide: States, Principalities, Population Movements and Interactions in Ethiopia (13th – Mid-16th Century)


## 1\. The Restored "Solomonic" Dynasty (1270s–1550s)

In 1270, a major power shift ended the Zagwe dynasty and established a ruling class claiming direct descent from the Biblical King Solomon.

### 👑 State Structure & Legitimacy

```
[THE TRIANGLE OF MEDIEVAL POWER]
                     Neguse Negest 
                    (Supreme Power)
                      /        \
                     /          \
      Church & Clergy <──────────> Regional Lords (Mesafint)
     (Ideological Support)         (Controlled Gult & Armies)
                     \          /
                      \        /
                    Peasantry (Gebebar)
                   (Tax/Tribute & Labor)
```
- **Yikuno Amlak (r. 1270–1285):** Overthrew the last Zagwe king. To justify his takeover, he claimed descent from **Dil Naod** (the last Aksumite king) and the union of King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.
- **The Kibre Negest ("Glory of Kings"):** A 14th-century epic that codified this Solomonic lineage, making it a sacred political doctrine that remained in place until 1974.
- **Roving Capitals:** The kingdom lacked a permanent, built capital. The royal court lived in massive tent cities that moved regularly.
	- **Motive:** Enabled the king to directly monitor provincial governors, collect food and tribute in person, and prevent local rebellions.
- **The Gult System:** A land tenure system where the king granted rights to collect tribute from peasant farmers (*Gebebar*) to military officers and administrative chiefs in place of salaries.

### ⚔️ Consolidation and Expansion

- **The Amba Geshen Prison:** To prevent succession wars, all male descendants of the king (except the direct heir) were confined to the flat-topped mountain prison of **Amba Geshen** in Wollo.
- **Amde Tsiyon (r. 1314–1344):** The most militaristic king of the era. He conquered Damot, Gojjam, and several southern territories.
	- **Core Objective:** To establish a complete **monopoly** over the highly profitable **Zeyla trade routes**.
- **Monastic Expansion:** Monks like **Abba Tekle Haymanot** established influential monasteries (such as Debre Libanos), spreading Christianity and extending Solomonic political influence south of the Abay River.

## 2\. The Rise of Muslim Sultanates & Trade Networks

Islamic states in the Horn of Africa developed primarily along the commercial highways connecting the Red Sea/Gulf of Aden to the rich interior.

## 3\. Western, Southern, and Central States

These highly structured societies in south and southwest Ethiopia possessed independent economies, distinct religious systems, and advanced agricultural methods.

- **Kingdom of Gojjam:** Dominated by the Agew people. Highly fertile and agricultural; periodically paid tribute to the Christian kingdom but retained strong administrative autonomy.
- **Ennarya:** Located in the Gibe region and ruled by a king titled the **Moti**. It was the primary source of premium export goods like **Gold** and **Civet**.
- **Kingdom of Kafa:** A highly centralized and isolated state ruled by the **Tato**.
	- **Defenses:** Built a massive network of deep ditches and earthwork walls called **Kello** to protect its borders.
		- **Heritage:** The legendary home of wild coffee (*Coffea arabica*).
- **Kingdom of Wolayta:** Located in the fertile Omo River valley.
	- **The Enset Engine:** Intensive cultivation of **Enset** ("false banana") provided unprecedented food security, allowing Wolayta to support one of the densest populations in the Horn of Africa.

## 4\. The 16th-Century Christian-Adal Conflict

This decade-long total war shattered the balance of power in the region and turned a local trade rivalry into a global proxy conflict.

### 📅 Chronology of Conflict

```
1529 AD: Battle of Shimbra Kure 
 (Imam Ahmed's Adalite forces crush King Lebne Dengel's army using superior mobility)
   │
 1530s AD: Adalite Conquest of Highlands 
 (Imam Ahmed controls the highlands; burns medieval monasteries and libraries)
   │
 1541 AD: Portuguese Intervention 
 (400 Portuguese musketeers led by Christopher da Gama land at Massawa)
   │
 1543 AD: Battle of Wayna Daga 
 (King Gelawdewos & Portuguese defeat Adalites; Imam Ahmed is killed)
   │
 1559 AD: Second Phase & Mutual Suicide 
 (Emir Nur Mujahid of Adal defeats Gelawdewos; both major states are left exhausted)
```

### 🌍 The Global Proxy Factor

- **The Ottoman Empire:** Supplied Adal with firearms (matchlock muskets) and elite mercenaries to secure their influence over Red Sea shipping lanes.
- **The Portuguese Kingdom:** Provided military assistance to the Christian kingdom to secure an allied Christian presence in the Indian Ocean.
- **The Legacy:** The war resulted in massive loss of life, the destruction of priceless cultural heritage, and a complete power vacuum in the central highlands.

## 5\. Population Movements

The mid-16th century saw major demographic shifts driven by war, environmental changes, and population growth.

### 🐪 Somali, Afar, and Argoba Movements

- **Argoba:** Dispersed along the eastern escarpment because their homeland lay directly in the path of the destructive Christian-Adalite war.
- **Afar:** Moved toward the Middle Awash basin due to persistent low-land droughts.

### 🌿 The Oromo Population Movement

The Oromo migration was the most influential demographic event of the 16th century, permanently changing the ethnic, linguistic, and political map of Ethiopia.

- **The Causes:**
	1. **Demographic Pressure:** Rapid growth of human and livestock populations in the wet highlands of **Madda Walabu** (Bale-Sidama border).
		2. **Environmental:** Severe droughts in the lowlands forced movements toward the wetter highlands.
		3. **Political Opportunity:** The "mutual exhaustion" of the Christian and Adalite armies left the highlands completely undefended.
- **The Gadaa System (The Engine of Expansion):**
	- Provided a democratic political, social, and military structure.
		- **Butta Wars:** Every 8 years, a new Gadaa warrior class was required to initiate military campaigns (**Butta**) to prove their combat readiness.
- **Social Integration Systems:**
	- **Mogasa:** The collective adoption and integration of conquered clans/tribes into Oromo lineages, granting them equal social and legal status.
		- **Gudifacha:** The individual adoption of children.
		- **Impact:** Allowed the Oromo to grow rapidly in numbers and assimilate surrounding populations as they moved.

### 📉 Long-Term Consequences

1. **Linguistic & Cultural Synthesis:** Wide-scale integration, language adoption, and cultural mixing across the central and southern highlands.
2. **Political Transformation:** Ancient southern kingdoms like Damot, Bizamo, and Ennarya were either permanently dissolved or structurally modified.
3. **State Relocation:** The Christian kingdom abandoned its traditional political center in Shewa and moved northwest to the Lake Tana basin, paving the way for the **Gondarine Period**.

**🎯 Exam Tip:** Understand the functional difference between **Mogasa** (mass adoption of entire groups) and **Gudifacha** (individual adoption of a child). Keep in mind that the **Butta** military ritual occurred strictly in **8-year cycles** governed by the **Gadaa** calendar.
