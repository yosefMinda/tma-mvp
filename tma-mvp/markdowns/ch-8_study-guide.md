# Unit 8 Study Guide: Political, Social and Economic Processes in Ethiopia Mid- 16 to Mid- 19 C.


## 1\. Peoples and States of Southern, Western and Eastern Ethiopia

The southern and southwestern regions of Ethiopia featured a diverse political landscape, ranging from divine monarchies to age-grade councils.

### 🗺️ The Southern Omotic & Cushitic State Systems

```
[OMOTIC SYSTEMS: DIVINE MONARCHY]          [CUSHITIC SYSTEMS: ROTATIONAL & DEMOCRATIC]
             - Tato (Kafa) / Kawo (Wolaita)               - Sera-Woma System (Sidama, Kambata, Hadiya)
             - Council of Ministers (Mikkerecho)          - Luwa/Ballee Rotational Councils
             - Defended by massive stone walls            - Governed by lineage customary laws (Gurage)
```

#### Comparison of Divine Kingship Systems

- **Kafa (Tato System):** Led by the divine **Tato**, who was structurally checked by the **Mikkerecho** (Council of Seven). The council held the absolute power to choose the king's successor.
	- *Defense:* Utilized the **Hiriyoo** (massive networks of defensive dry-trenches).
- **Wolaita (Kawo System):** Led by the absolute **Kawo**. Its high-density population was sustained by intensive **Enset** farming.
	- *Defense:* Constructed the **Ijajo Kalla** (Wall of Ijajo), a series of defensive dry-stone walls.
- **Yem:** Ruled by the **Mowa** dynasty; the king (*Amno*) acted as both a political administrator and high priest.
- **Dawuro:** Famous for the **Kati Halala Kella** (walls of Kati Halala) built to defend against external incursions.
- **Gamo:** Structured into autonomous administrative units called **Dere**, where supreme power resided in the **Duletha** (general assembly of the people).

#### Cushitic Age-Grade & Assembly Governance

- **The Sidama & the Luwa System:** Utilized the **Luwa** system, an age-grade structure where executive power rotated every **8 years** across five generation sets (e.g., *Wawassa, Darara*), preventing political dictatorial dynasties.
- **The Kambata & Hadiya:** Governed by the unwritten **Sera** (customary laws) and a supreme assembly called the **Hambarcho**.
- **The Konso:** Renowned for **Stone-walled Terraces** and fortified hilltop villages (*Paletas*). They used the **Tsella** generation set for social and labor organization.

#### The Segmentary Lineage Systems

- **The Gurage:** Resisted centralized kingship; instead, they were governed by lineage-based customary codes. Legislative decisions were settled by the **Yajoka Qicha** (among the Sebat Bet Gurage) and the **Gordanna Sera** (among the Kistane).
- **The Siltie:** Administered by the **YeSiltie Sera** under the leadership of the **Mule Gerad**.

## 2\. Western States & The Transformation of Gadaa

In the 18th and 19th centuries, the elective, rotational **Gadaa system** of the Macha Oromo transitioned into hereditary monarchies.

```
[ELECTIVE GADAA SYSTEM]
   │
   ├──> Frequent expansionist conflicts require permanent military commanders (Abba Duulaa)
   ├──> Accumulation of wealth from the revival of long-distance trade routes
   ├──> Ideological influence of Islam (providing a centralized alternative to Gadaa)
   │
 [HEREDITARY MONARCHIES: THE MOTI SYSTEM]
```

### ☕ The Five Gibe States & Wollega Kingdoms

1. **Limmu-Ennarya:** The earliest of the Gibe states; dominated regional trade under **Abba Bagibo**.
2. **Jimma:** Reached peak prosperity under **Abba Jifar I**; became a major center for Islamic law, scholarship, and coffee commerce.
3. **Guma, Gomma, and Gera:** Strategic forest states that prospered through the export of civet, gold, and coffee.
4. **Leqa Naqamte:** Founded by **Bakare Godana**; grew wealthy through agriculture and trade.
5. **Leqa Qellem:** Founded by **Tullu**; dominated gold trade routes along the Sudanese border.

## 3\. Eastern States: The Fortress City-State

### 🧱 The Emirate of Harar & The Jugol Wall

- **Origin:** Re-established as an independent, sovereign Emirate in 1647 under **Emir Ali Dawud**.
- **The Jugol Wall:** Constructed in the mid-16th century by **Emir Nur Mujahid** to protect the city against Oromo expansion.

- **Regional Neighbors:**
	- **Afar Sultanates:** Controlled the shipping corridors from the coast to the interior. The **Aussa Sultanate** was the most powerful.
		- **The Somali Clans:** Decentralized societies governed by customary legal codes (**Heer**) and a council of elders (**Guurti**).

## 4\. Conflict over Catholicism & The Jesuit Mission

In the early 17th century, the Solomonic court attempted to secure Portuguese firearms by converting to Roman Catholicism, triggering a bloody civil war.

### ⚔️ The Conversion Crisis

```
[JESUIT MISSIONARIES ARRIVE] ──> Pedro Paez wins King Susinyos' trust (1622 Conversion)
                                       │
                                       ├──> Alfonso Mendez enforces radical Latin reforms
                                       ├──> Banning of Sabbath & Orthodox fasting calendars
                                       │
 [BLOODY CIVIL WAR] <──────────────────┘
   │
   ├──> Susinyos realizes the destruction, abdicates in favor of Fasiledes (1632)
   │
 [RESTORATION & THE CLOSED-DOOR POLICY]
```
- **Pedro Paez:** Strategic, patient missionary who studied local languages and cultures to convert the elite.
- **Alfonso Mendez:** Rigid Latin extremist who ordered the rebaptism of Orthodox Christians and banned local traditions, leading to the collapse of social peace.
- **The Closed-Door Policy (1632):** Introduced by **King Fasiledes**. He expelled the Jesuits, executed Catholic sympathizers, and banned Europeans from entering the highlands, establishing defensive alliances with Ottoman pashas at Massawa to block Catholic entry.

## 5\. The Gondarine Period (1636–1769)

By establishing Gondar as a permanent, stone-built capital in 1636, King Fasiledes ended the era of rorating tent capitals.

- **Fasil Ghebbi:** The imperial compound containing spectacular multi-story stone castles blending Portuguese, Indian, and local Aksumite architectural styles.
- **The Cultural Golden Age:** Advanced the arts, manuscript illumination, classical Ge'ez poetry (*Qene*), and religious chanting (*Yaredic Zema*).
- **The Great Monarchs:** **Fasiledes**, **Yohannis I** (who enforced strict religious segregation laws), and **Iyasu I** (the great reformer).
- **Empress Mintiwab:** Governed as a powerful regent through her family from Quara, introducing Wollo and Yejju Oromo leaders into the Gondarine court to balance regional rivalries.

## 6\. Zemene Mesafint: The Era of Warlords (1769–1855)

The Era of Warlords was a period of complete political fragmentation, marking the collapse of centralized Solomonic authority.

- **The Catalyst (1769):** **Ras Mikael Sehul** of Tigray marched on Gondar and assassinated **King Iyoas I**, shattering the traditional "divine right" of the monarch.
- **The Yejju Dynasty (Wara Sheh):** A dynasty of Oromo lords who acted as the actual rulers (*Regents*) of the empire. They governed from their capital at **Debre Tabor**, while keeping impoverished, puppet Solomonic kings on the throne at Gondar.
- **Socio-Economic Collapse:** Constant campaigns destroyed the peasantry's farms, and arbitrary taxes at regional custom checkpoints (*Kellas*) stagnated long-distance trade.

## 8\. European Travelers & Imperial Motives

In the 19th century, a wave of European scientific explorers entered Ethiopia.

- **The Figures:** Explorers like **James Bruce** (who traced the Blue Nile), **Henry Salt**, and **Walter Plowden**.
- **The Hidden Motive:** While presenting themselves as scientists, geographers, or humanitarians, they were gathering vital geographical, resource, and military intelligence for their home governments to facilitate the eventual Scramble for Africa.
