# Unit 9 Study Guide: The Industrial Revolution and Revolutionary Movements

## 1\. The Industrial Revolution (Mid-18th Century)

The Industrial Revolution marked the transition from agrarian, hand-craft economies to automated, machine-driven manufacturing systems.

```
[THE INDUSTRIAL REVOLUTION ENGINE]
   Abundant Coal & Iron ---> Steam Engine (James Watt) ---> Factory System
   Accumulated Capital  ---> Spinning Jenny / Power Loom -> Mass Textilisation
   Property Rights      ---> Steam Locomotives / Ships  ---> Global Trade Expansion
```

### 🌍 Why Britain First?

- **Natural Endowments:** Rich deposits of **Coal** (for power generation) and **Iron Ore** (for structural metallurgy) situated close to water transport.
- **Capital Wealth:** Enormous merchant capital reserves accumulated from trans-Atlantic slave-trading and Asian maritime monopolies.
- **Institutions:** Stable constitutional monarchy guaranteeing private property rights, free enterprise, and patent protections.

### 👤 The Class Stratification of Industrial Capitalism

| Social Class | Key Definition / Role | Living & Working Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Bourgeoisie** | The middle-class owners of the means of production (factories, mines, banks). | Accumulated immense wealth; eventually replaced the old agrarian aristocracy as the ruling political elite. |
| **The Proletariat** | The industrial working class who owned no factories and sold their physical labor for wages. | Overcrowded, unsanitary urban tenement housing (slums);  $$ 12 $$ \- $$ 16 $$  hour workdays; systemic exposure to toxic environments; extensive exploitation of child and female labor. |

### 📈 Global Consequence: Imperialism

Industrialization increased the demand for raw agricultural/mineral materials (cotton, rubber, palm oil) and domestic trade surpluses. This drove European powers to militarily colonize Africa and Asia in search of secured markets.

## 2\. The American War of Independence (1775–1783)

The American Revolution was an anti-colonial struggle driven by tax disputes and the constitutional ideals of the European Enlightenment.

```
[CHRONOLOGY OF AMERICAN INDEPENDENCE]
 1773: Boston Tea Party (Protest dumping of East India Company tea)
   │
 1775: Outbreak of Military Hostilities 
   │
 July 4, 1776: Declaration of Independence (Drafted by Thomas Jefferson)
   │
 1783: Treaty of Paris (Britain recognizes sovereign USA)
   │
 1787: Constitution Framed (Implements Separation of Powers & Bill of Rights)
```

### 🧠 Intellectual Foundations

- **John Locke (Social Contract):** Argued that governments are only legitimate if they protect citizens' natural rights (**Life, Liberty, and Property**). If a government violates these, the citizens have a right to overthrow it.
- **Taxation without Representation:** The core political grievance—colonists argued that Britain had no right to levy internal taxes (such as the Stamp Act) because the colonists had no elected representatives in the British Parliament.
- **The Victory Factors:** Effective revolutionary military leadership under **George Washington**, combined with critical military alliances and financial backing from **France** and Spain.

## 3\. The French Revolution (1789–1799)

Unlike the American Revolution, the French Revolution was a radical, internal social upheaval that dismantled the ancient feudal system in Europe.

### ⚖️ The Inequity of the Three Estates (The Old Regime)

```
[THE THREE ESTATES OF FRANCE]
                
                     /\   1. First Estate: Clergy (0.5% Pop)
                    /  \  - Owned 10% land; Paid ZERO taxes.
                   /____\
                  /      \  2. Second Estate: Nobility (1.5% Pop)
                 /________\ - Held all high state offices; Paid ZERO taxes.
                /          \
               /            \ 3. Third Estate: Everyone Else (98% Pop)
              /______________\- Peasants, Urban Workers, Bourgeoisie.
                              - Paid 100% of national taxes; Held zero power.
```

### 📅 Major Milestones of the Revolution

- **The Estates-General Crisis (1789):** Financial bankruptcy forced King Louis XVI to convene the assembly. The Third Estate broke away, declaring themselves the sovereign **National Assembly**.
- **Storming of the Bastille (July 14, 1789):** Armed Parisian citizens attacked and dismantled the royal prison, signaling the popular, violent breakdown of absolute royal authority.
- **Declaration of the Rights of Man & Citizen:** Declared that all human beings are born free and remain equal in civic rights under the watchword: *"Liberty, Equality, Fraternity."*
- **The Reign of Terror (1793–1794):** Under the radical leadership of **Maximilien Robespierre** and the Jacobins, the **Committee of Public Safety** executed over 
	$$
	17,000
	$$
	 alleged "internal enemies" of the revolution using the **Guillotine**, including King Louis XVI and Queen Marie Antoinette.

## 4\. The Era of Napoleon & The Congress of Vienna

```
1799: Napoleon executes a Coup d'État, seizing control of the state
   │
 1804: Crowns himself Emperor; enacts the historic Napoleonic Code
   │
 1812: Disastrous Russian Campaign (600,000 troops decimated by winter cold)
   │
 1815: Final military defeat at the Battle of Waterloo
   │
 1815: Congress of Vienna restores the old borders and monarchies
```

### 📕 The Reforms of Napoleon Bonaparte

- **The Napoleonic Code (Civil Code):** Replaced thousands of local feudal customs with a single, uniform system of laws. It guaranteed legal equality for all male citizens, secularized the state, and protected property rights.
- **Administrative Centralization:** Founded the National Bank, stabilized currency, reorganized the taxation system, and established state-run high schools (**Lycées**).

### 🏛️ The Congress of Vienna (1815 AD)

Following the defeat of Napoleon, European diplomats gathered in Austria to rebuild the continent.

- **Key Principles:**
	1. **Legitimacy:** Re-establishing the hereditary royal dynasties overthrown by Napoleon back onto their thrones.
		2. **Balance of Power:** Redrawing borders and building buffer states to ensure no single nation could ever dominate Europe again.
- **The Evaluation:** While it successfully prevented a continent-wide war for nearly a century, it failed to suppress the long-term growth of **Nationalism** and **Democracy** triggered by the French Revolution.

**🎯 Exam Tip:** Focus on the chronological transition of revolutionary ideas: *Enlightenment → American Revolution → French Revolution*. Always remember that **Britain** was the birthplace of industrialization, the **Bastille** was stormed on **July 14, 1789**, and Napoleon met his final military defeat at **Waterloo** in **1815**.
